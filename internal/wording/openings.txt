# The openings that say what a line is, where no heading placed it.
#
# Synonym rules, as headings.txt has: phrases => the kind of section a
# line that opens with one belongs in. A phrase is found only at the start
# of a line, past any bullet or figure ("33 days holiday"), as whole
# words, in any case. The longest phrase wins, so "you'll have" is a
# requirement and "you'll get" a benefit, though "you'll" alone is
# neither: "You'll be…" can be either.

experience of, experience in, experience with, experience working,
	previous experience, proven experience, proven, must have, you must have,
	you will have, you'll have, ability to, able to, knowledge of,
	understanding of, qualified, a qualification in, minimum,
	full uk driving licence, full uk driving license, uk driving licence,
	driving licence, driving license, a full driving licence,
	valid driving licence, enhanced dbs, the right to work => requirements

responsible for, you will be responsible for, duties include,
	carry out, carrying out, manage, managing, lead, deliver, delivering,
	ensure, ensuring, oversee, overseeing, conduct, conducting, prepare,
	preparing, maintain, maintaining, monitor, monitoring, coordinate,
	coordinating, assist with, assisting with, undertake, liaise with,
	liaising with => responsibilities

free parking, on site parking, pension, company pension, pension scheme,
	days holiday, days annual leave, annual leave, holiday allowance,
	annual bonus, bonus scheme, private healthcare, private medical,
	healthcare, life assurance, life insurance, cycle to work,
	employee discounts, employee assistance, company sick pay, sick pay,

	you'll receive, you'll have the opportunity,
